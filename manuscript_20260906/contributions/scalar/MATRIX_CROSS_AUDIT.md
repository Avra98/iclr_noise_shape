# Independent cross-audit of the matrix module

Reviewed `../matrix/matrix_main.tex`, `matrix_appendix.tex`, and `highdim_example.tex` without editing them. Also checked the primary statement of Alsmeyer (1997), Theorem1, at https://www.uni-muenster.de/Stochastik/alsmeyer/MRTheorem2.pdf, printed page5.

## Verdict

The exact regenerative matrix theorem and the high-dimensional SGD counterexample have coherent, sufficient assumptions and valid proof mechanisms. The newly derived uniform potential bound repairs an important gap in the earlier drafts. The signed nonlinear-source formulation is appropriately conditional and does not smuggle in a universal central-density conclusion.

One substantive wording repair is needed in the main discussion of ambient densities: a radial density asymptotic plus a regular limiting angular measure does not alone imply a pointwise joint/ambient density asymptotic. Require **joint local density convergence** in radius and direction, with the relevant angular density, before dividing by the polar Jacobian and writing p_U(rs) proportional to r^(α−d). Alternatively keep that statement at the level of small-ball/angular measures. Tiny angular spikes can violate pointwise density convergence while preserving the measure limit.

## Checks

1. **Uniform interval domination.** Under the tilt, the moment E_hat exp(t(Z_n−Z_0)) equals P_(−α+t)^n h/h. A spectral radius strictly below1 and bounded positive h give a uniform Cρ^n bound. From inside an interval of width b, a return at time n entails Z_n−Z_0≥−b; exponential Markov gives e^(tb)Cρ^n. Summation is finite. Starting outside and applying the bound at first entrance preserves uniformity over the whole radius/direction space. This proof is correct and does not require independence of scalar gains.

2. **Alsmeyer theorem match.** The primary theorem assumes a Harris recurrent modulating chain, nonarithmetic additive increments, finite positive drift, and a directly Riemann integrable type radial test. Applying it to −Z under the tilt is legitimate. The test 1_D(s)e^(αu)/h(s), restricted to a finite interval in u, is bounded, integrable, and continuous in u except at the two endpoints. The theorem initially supplies the limit for stationary-angular almost every starting direction, as the module states.

3. **All-start extension.** The proof must be understood as acting on the *tilted* potential tested against e^(α(Z_n−x))/h(S_n). This test is uniformly bounded on the moving interval. After one step the direction law is absolutely continuous with respect to the tilted invariant direction law, so the almost-every-direction limit applies. The proven uniform interval bound dominates the conditional potential even when the first log increment is unbounded. Thus dominated convergence is valid without assuming an extra inverse moment of the first increment. The existing text describes this correctly; an explicit tested-potential formula could make it easier to audit.

4. **Entry/exit averaging.** The unrestricted original potential is bounded by C e^(αx−αz)h(s). A finite entry inverse α-moment and bounded h dominate entries. Upper exit has Z_tau≥0, so its weight is bounded. Subtracting unrestricted potential after exit is valid. The killed leading coefficient can vanish; the survival-probability identity is the correct way to impose positivity. It must not be inferred merely from increasing endpoint radius.

5. **Reflection example.** For A=M(I−2vv^T), products are a scalar product times an orthogonal matrix, so the norm gain and all pressures reduce exactly to M. Reflections are symmetric and generally noncommuting. The transition density of the direction relative to uniform surface measure is ||s−s'||^(2−d); the constants are correct, including d=2 where the transition is uniform. The lower bound 2^(2−d) gives the stated uniform mixing and absolute continuity. Under the inverse tilt, the direction kernel is unchanged. The bounded continuous (piecewise) scalar gain law is nonarithmetic and has a unique inverse root larger than1. The stopped-martingale bound P_hat(exit)≤exp(−α) and Wald bound on the original exit time are valid. The sample Hessian is symmetric; positive-definite population Hessian is correctly restricted to d=2 in the stated argument.

6. **Conditional nonlinear source.** G=ν+GQ is valid when complete regenerative cycle endpoints lie outside the local domain: all local successors remain counted until actual exit. Thus J=ν+G(Q−L0) is a finite signed source and G=J+GL0. The G-almost-everywhere exit hypothesis makes GL0^n vanish in total variation. Weighted total variation integrability of J permits interchange with the killed resolvent and passage to the central limit. Positivity of its renewal coefficient is separately assumed, which is necessary. The proposed V_p bound is an independent sufficient integrability criterion, not the desired density conclusion in disguise. Nearby deterministic maps do not satisfy the weighted total variation discrepancy merely because their values are close; the module correctly warns about that.

7. **High-dimensional exact SGD.** The stated map differentiates the displayed loss correctly. Both chord lower bounds on log gains have the right inequality direction. Conditional gain distributions are independent of the past by isotropy; the finite-basis/Frobenius comparison correctly upgrades the fixed-vector exponent to the top operator exponent. Positivity plus the constant eigenfunction identifies the spectral radius for every real power. The determinant identity gives k(−d)=7/8 exactly. The remainder bound sqrt(3ρ/8), second-moment contraction factor1−43/(64d), and noise contribution19σ²/8 are correct. The rank-one Gaussian noise averaged over random directions has the displayed full-dimensional positive density off the diagonal, so compact minorization and uniqueness follow. For d≥3 the conditional projection densities are centrally nonincreasing, and mixtures preserve that property. The model is nonquadratic but convex, not the separate nonconvex example; the final paragraph correctly says so.

## Presentation safeguards

- Call this a central renewal application or extension of the *renewal framework with nonlinear return*, not an affine Kesten stationary-law theorem beyond positive global Lyapunov drift.
- Avoid claiming novelty for standard queue, Markov renewal, or Harris arguments; the concrete comparison and verified constructions are the manuscript's synthesis.
- A scalar Rayleigh gain is not P_q, and its estimated log mean is not automatically a top matrix-product exponent.
- At fixed nondegenerate additive noise the exact zero-radius multiplicative law generally fails. The source proposition is an exact conditional theorem, not a verified neural-network hypothesis.
